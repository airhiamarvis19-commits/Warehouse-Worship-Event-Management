# Stakeholder Management Plan – Warehouse Worship: The Gathering 2026

## 1. Purpose

This stakeholder management plan identifies the key stakeholder groups that would be involved in planning and delivering a large-scale worship event.

The stakeholder structure below is a proposed project-management model created for this portfolio case study. It does not claim to represent Warehouse Worship's actual internal management structure.

---

---

## 3. Power–Interest Approach

Stakeholders can be grouped according to their level of influence and interest.

### Manage Closely

High influence and high interest stakeholders should receive regular communication and be directly involved in important decisions.

Examples:

- Event leadership
- Venue management
- Production suppliers
- Security team
- Artists / programme team
- Medical and safety providers

### Keep Satisfied

Stakeholders with high influence but comparatively lower day-to-day interest should receive appropriate updates and escalation where required.

Examples:

- Local authorities
- Emergency services
- Key partners

### Keep Informed

Stakeholders with high interest but lower influence should receive clear and timely information.

Examples:

- Attendees
- Volunteers
- General event staff
- Marketing team

### Monitor

Stakeholders with relatively low influence and lower interest should receive proportionate communication.

Examples:

- Some secondary suppliers
- Wider external stakeholders

## 4. Communication Plan

| Stakeholder Group | Information Required | Communication Method | Frequency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Project leadership | Progress, budget, risks and decisions | Project meetings / reports | Weekly |
| Venue | Site requirements, access and event operations | Meetings / email | Weekly during delivery |
| Production suppliers | Technical requirements and schedules | Production meetings | Weekly, increasing near event |
| Artists / programme team | Programme, rehearsal and performance requirements | Meetings / email | As required |
| Security | Crowd plan, access, emergency procedures | Operational meetings | Regularly before event |
| Staff / volunteers | Roles, responsibilities and event-day procedures | Briefings / training | Before and on event day |
| Attendees | Timings, venue, ticketing and event information | Email / social media / website | At key milestones |
| Transport providers | Departure times, routes and passenger requirements | Email / operational meetings | As required |
| Medical providers | Medical arrangements and emergency procedures | Safety meetings | Before event |
| Partners / sponsors | Progress and agreed deliverables | Reports / meetings | At agreed milestones |

## 5. RACI Framework

A RACI model can be used to clarify responsibility.

**R = Responsible**  
**A = Accountable**  
**C = Consulted**  
**I = Informed**

| Activity | Project Lead | Venue | Production | Security | Marketing | Programme |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Project planning | A/R | C | C | C | C | C |
| Venue planning | A | R | C | C | I | I |
| Programme planning | A | I | C | I | C | R |
| Production planning | A | C | R | C | I | C |
| Marketing campaign | A | I | I | I | R | C |
| Ticketing | A/R | C | I | I | C | I |
| Security planning | A | C | C | R | I | I |
| Staffing | A/R | C | C | C | I | I |
| Event-day operations | A | R | R | R | C | C |
| Post-event evaluation | A/R | C | C | C | C | C |

---

## 6. Stakeholder Engagement Principles

Effective stakeholder management would be based on:

- Clear ownership of responsibilities
- Timely communication
- Defined escalation routes
- Accurate project information
- Regular progress monitoring
- Early identification of conflicts
- Documented decisions
- Appropriate communication frequency
- Feedback from relevant stakeholder groups

---

## 7. Potential Stakeholder Risks

| Risk | Potential Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Unclear responsibilities | Duplication or missed tasks | Use RACI and defined role descriptions |
| Poor communication | Delays and operational errors | Establish regular communication channels |
| Supplier misunderstanding | Technical or delivery issues | Use detailed briefs and confirmation meetings |
| Programme changes | Marketing and operational disruption | Maintain change-control procedures |
| Staff information gaps | Inconsistent attendee experience | Provide structured briefings |
| Conflicting stakeholder priorities | Delayed decisions | Establish escalation and decision-making routes |
| Late decisions | Pressure on delivery timeline | Set clear approval deadlines |

---

## 8. Stakeholder Review

Stakeholder requirements should be reviewed throughout the project rather than treated as static.

Changes to the programme, venue, attendance expectations, suppliers or operational arrangements may change stakeholder priorities and communication requirements.

The project manager should therefore review the stakeholder register at key project milestones and update engagement strategies where necessary.

---

## 9. Case Study Limitation

The stakeholder groups, RACI assignments and engagement methods presented in this document are **proposed project-management structures developed for portfolio purposes**.

They are not presented as Warehouse Worship's actual internal organisational structure or stakeholder responsibilities.
