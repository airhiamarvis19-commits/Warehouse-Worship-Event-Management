# Lessons Learned – Warehouse Worship: The Gathering 2026

## 1. Purpose

This lessons-learned document evaluates the project-management and event-delivery observations identified through this independent case study.

The purpose is to identify what appeared to work effectively, where operational pressure was observed, and what project-management improvements could be considered for future large-scale events.

This analysis combines publicly available event information with first-hand attendee observation.

It does **not** claim access to Warehouse Worship's internal post-event evaluation or lessons-learned documentation.

---

## 2. Evidence Used

The lessons in this document are based on three evidence categories:

| Evidence Type | Description |
|---|---|
| Published / Verified Information | Public event information and published event coverage |
| First-Hand Observation | The author's personal experience attending the event |
| Project Analysis | Illustrative project-management interpretation based on the evidence |

This distinction is important because observations and recommendations should not be presented as confirmed internal findings.

## 3. Overall Reflection

The event provided a useful case study for examining how project-management principles translate into a live, large-scale event environment.

From an attendee perspective, the event involved:

- Significant attendee numbers
- A structured entry process
- Security screening
- Ticket scanning
- Wristband allocation
- Seating and standing areas
- Multiple event facilities
- Large-scale production
- Crowd movement
- Staff communication
- Public transport considerations

The experience demonstrates how several project workstreams must operate together to create a successful attendee journey.

---

## 4. Lesson 1 – Entry Planning Is Critical

### Observation

The author's first-hand experience involved a significant queue on arrival and an overall entry process lasting approximately one hour or slightly longer.

The process included:

**Queue → Security → Ticket Scan → Wristband → Venue Entry**

### Project-Management Lesson

Entry should be treated as a major operational workstream rather than simply a final event-day activity.

Planning should consider:

- Expected arrival patterns
- Security throughput
- Ticket-scanning capacity
- Queue capacity
- Staffing levels
- Entry routes
- Attendee communication

### Future Improvement

A future project could use estimated arrival curves and throughput calculations to determine the resources required at each stage of entry.

---

## 5. Lesson 2 – Clear Signage Supports Attendee Flow

### Observation

The author observed that signage was generally easy to follow during the event.

### Project-Management Lesson

Effective wayfinding can reduce the number of attendees requiring assistance and can support smoother movement around the venue.

### Future Improvement

Signage should be reviewed from the perspective of a first-time attendee.

Key decision points should be identified before event day.

---

## 6. Lesson 3 – Staff Communication Is Important

### Observation

The author observed clear announcements and communication from staff during the event.

### Project-Management Lesson

Large events require consistent communication between operational teams and attendees.

Staff should know:

- What information they are responsible for providing
- Who they report to
- How to escalate problems
- Where key facilities are located
- What to do during an emergency

### Future Improvement

A standardised staff briefing and communication structure should be implemented before doors open.

---

## 7. Lesson 4 – Crowd Management Must Be Continuous

### Observation

The author perceived the internal crowd environment as relatively controlled, with staff directing attendee movement.

### Project-Management Lesson

Crowd management should not stop once attendees enter the venue.

Crowd conditions can change throughout the programme and should therefore be continuously monitored.

### Future Improvement

Use predefined crowd-monitoring points and escalation thresholds.

- Suppliers

## 10. Lesson 7 – Risk Management Should Be Linked to Operations

The risk register identified several operational risks including:

- Entry congestion
- Crowd congestion
- Medical incidents
- Emergency situations
- Technical failure
- Supplier delays
- Attendee confusion

These risks are directly connected to operational planning.

### Project-Management Lesson

Risk registers should not exist as standalone documents.

Risk controls should be reflected in:

- The schedule
- Staffing plan
- Operations plan
- Run of Show
- Supplier requirements
- Event-day procedures

---

## 11. Lesson 8 – Stakeholder Coordination Is Essential

Large-scale events involve multiple stakeholder groups.

These may include:

- Event leadership
- Venue management
- Production
- Security
- Artists
- Suppliers
- Volunteers
- Attendees
- Partners

### Project-Management Lesson

Stakeholder responsibilities should be defined early to reduce ambiguity.

The stakeholder plan developed for this case study uses:

- Power–Interest analysis
- RACI responsibility allocation
- Communication planning
- Escalation processes

---

## 12. Lesson 9 – Supplier Management Affects Delivery

Production, security, transport, catering and other specialist services may depend on external suppliers.

### Project-Management Lesson

Supplier management should extend beyond appointment.

It should include:

**Specification → Procurement → Contract → Mobilisation → Delivery → Monitoring → Close-Out**

### Future Improvement

Supplier milestones should be linked directly to the master project schedule.

---

## 13. Lesson 10 – Contingency Should Be Planned

The illustrative budget included a 10% contingency allowance.

This demonstrates the importance of allowing for uncertainty in event projects.

Potential unexpected costs could arise from:

- Technical requirements
- Staffing
- Transport
- Supplier changes
- Emergency requirements
- Scope changes

### Project-Management Lesson

Contingency should be controlled rather than treated as unrestricted spending.

---

## 14. Lesson 11 – Data Should Drive Evaluation

The KPI dashboard developed for this case study identified measures such as:

- Attendance
- Entry waiting time
- Ticket scanning
- Safety incidents
- Programme performance
- Technical failures
- Staff briefing completion
- Budget variance
- Stakeholder satisfaction

### Project-Management Lesson

A project should define how success will be measured before delivery rather than attempting to determine success retrospectively.

---

## 15. Lesson 12 – Do Not Confuse Observation With Verified Data

One of the most important lessons from this case study is the importance of evidence classification.

For example:

### First-Hand Observation

The author experienced a lengthy entry process.

### Published Information

Public event information confirms the event timings and published event details.

### Illustrative Analysis

The project schedule, budget, risk scores and KPI targets were developed as portfolio outputs.

### Internal Data

Actual attendance, financial performance and internal incident statistics were not available.

### Project-Management Lesson

A professional project report should clearly distinguish between:

**Fact → Observation → Assumption → Analysis → Recommendation**

This improves transparency and credibility.

## 17. Areas for Potential Improvement

Based on the case-study evidence, potential improvement areas include:

### Entry Throughput

Review the capacity of security and ticket-scanning operations.

### Queue Management

Monitor waiting times and crowd build-up before entry.

### Arrival Communication

Provide detailed pre-event guidance to help attendees prepare for arrival.

### Crowd Flow

Continue monitoring movement after attendees enter the venue.

### Attendee Journey Measurement

Collect structured data across the full attendee journey.

## 23. Final Reflection

The Gathering 2026 provided a useful real-world environment for considering how project-management principles operate within a complex event setting.

The most significant learning point from the attendee perspective was the importance of the **arrival and entry process**.

A successful event is not only determined by what happens during the main programme. The attendee experience begins before entering the venue and continues through departure.

From a project-management perspective, this reinforces the importance of:

- End-to-end planning
- Clear responsibilities
- Risk-based thinking
- Stakeholder coordination
- Operational readiness
- Performance measurement
- Continuous improvement

## 25. Conclusion

Lessons learned are valuable because they allow project teams to convert experience into improvements for future delivery.

This case study demonstrates how first-hand observations can be combined with published information and project-management analysis to identify potential improvements without presenting assumptions as verified organisational facts.

The key lessons identified include:

- The importance of detailed entry planning
- The value of clear signage
- The importance of staff communication
- The need for continuous crowd management
- The value of structured stakeholder coordination
- The importance of supplier management
- The need to integrate risk management with operations
- The importance of measurable KPIs
- The value of evidence-based evaluation

These lessons provide a foundation for improving the planning and delivery of future large-scale events.
