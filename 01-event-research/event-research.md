# 01 — Event Research

## 1. Event Overview

**Event:** Warehouse Worship – The Gathering 2026  
**Date:** 8 August 2026  
**Location:** Manchester Central Convention Complex, Manchester  
**Doors:** 4:30 PM  
**Event Time:** 5:00 PM – 11:00 PM  
**Organiser:** Warehouse Worship

Warehouse Worship – The Gathering 2026 was a large-scale worship and live music event held in Manchester.

The event combined live worship, music, preaching, prayer, guest performances and other audience-facing experiences. Published coverage also identifies a live-recording component and wider objectives connected with Warehouse Worship's music activities.

---

## 2. Event Purpose

The event formed part of Warehouse Worship's wider development of large-scale worship gatherings.

Published coverage describes Warehouse Worship as having developed from smaller Manchester-based gatherings into larger events and regional Encounter Night tours.

The event therefore had several apparent purposes:

- Create a large-scale corporate worship experience
- Bring together worshippers and music audiences
- Deliver live performances and ministry
- Provide opportunities for guest artists and ministers
- Capture new music through live recording
- Support the wider development of Warehouse Worship's music activities

These purposes are based on published event coverage and should not be interpreted as a direct statement of the organiser's internal project objectives.

---

## 3. Venue

The event took place at **Manchester Central Convention Complex**.

Published coverage describes the venue as a large, column-free hall with a vaulted roof and a central elevated stage.

The event used a central-stage configuration with screens positioned around the performance area, supporting visibility across the audience.

The audience area incorporated both seated and standing sections.

The venue therefore created specific operational requirements around:

- Crowd movement
- Seating and standing areas
- Entry and exit routes
- Wayfinding
- Security
- Stewarding
- Emergency access
- Production

## 4. Audience & Attendance

The event was promoted as a large-scale gathering.

Pre-event information referenced an anticipated attendance exceeding 10,000 people. However, an independently verified final attendance figure was not available for this case study.

Therefore:

> **This portfolio does not claim that exactly 10,000 people attended.**

The audience environment observed first-hand was very busy, with significant numbers of attendees arriving around the event start time.

The ticket documentation confirms the event date and scheduled start time.

## 6. Transport & Logistics

Transport formed part of the wider attendee journey.

Organised coach services were promoted for attendees travelling from different locations, while other attendees could use Manchester's public transport network.

### First-Hand Attendee Journey

My journey to the event involved:

**Bus → Shudehill → Connecting bus → Walk to venue**

I arrived at approximately 5:00 PM.

The venue was approximately a short walk from the final bus stop.

### Arrival Experience

The main operational challenge I personally experienced was the queue at entry.

I waited approximately one hour in the queue, with the overall entry process taking approximately 1 hour and 10 minutes.

This observation is used in the case study as evidence for analysing peak arrival demand and entry capacity.

## 7. Marketing & Promotion

The event was promoted through an integrated combination of event branding, artist promotion, digital communications and ticketing.

Key marketing considerations included:

- Event branding
- Artist announcements
- Social media promotion
- Ticket promotion
- Event information
- Pre-event attendee communications
- Transport information
- Event-day instructions

The marketing and communications process can be viewed as:

**Awareness → Interest → Consideration → Ticket Purchase → Attendance → Event Experience → Post-Event Engagement**

Marketing therefore had a direct relationship with operational planning because ticket demand influences expected attendance and arrival patterns.

## 8. Event Operations

### Entry

The attendee entry process involved:

**Queue → Ticket scanning → Security → Wristband → Venue entry**

I personally experienced a significant queue on arrival.

### Security

Security screening formed part of the entrance process.

Pre-event communications also provided information about prohibited items and bag restrictions.

### Crowd Management

Published coverage describes active management of crowd movement and maintenance of clear exit channels.

From my first-hand observation, the environment was very busy but appeared to be managed through staff direction and crowd movement controls.

### Wayfinding

I found the venue signage relatively easy to follow.

### Facilities

I observed the availability of:

- Toilets
- Merchandise
- Seated areas
- Standing areas
- Exit routes

### Communication

Announcements were clear from my perspective, and staff communicated instructions to attendees.

### Departure

Departure was relatively straightforward from my perspective.

I left the venue, waited approximately 10 minutes and then travelled away from the venue by bus.

## 11. Post-Event Evaluation

The case study evaluates the event through:

- First-hand attendee observation
- Published event coverage
- Operational analysis
- Stakeholder analysis
- Risk analysis
- Proposed KPIs

The most significant improvement opportunity identified from first-hand observation was entry congestion during peak arrival.

- Project plan
- Financial records
- Supplier contracts

- Post-event survey results

Where information was unavailable, proposed project-management documents were created as **illustrative portfolio outputs**.

The project therefore distinguishes between:

**Verified information**  
Information supported by available documentation or published sources.

**First-hand observation**  
Information personally observed during attendance.

**Planning assumptions**  
Proposed figures, processes or targets created for the portfolio.

---

## 13. Research Methodology

This case study combines publicly available event information, event documentation and first-hand attendee observation.

Public sources were used to establish information relating to the event, venue, programme and operational arrangements.

First-hand observations were used to evaluate the attendee journey and identify potential operational considerations.

Where official project data was unavailable, proposed budgets, schedules, risk registers, stakeholder plans and KPIs were developed as analytical outputs rather than representations of the organiser's internal documentation.

---

## 14. Key Research Finding

The research indicates that large-scale event management requires integration across multiple project workstreams.

The attendee experience can be represented as:

**Marketing**

↓

**Ticketing**

↓

**Transport**

↓

**Arrival**

↓

**Entry & Security**

↓

**Crowd Management**

↓

**Programme**

↓

**Facilities**

↓

**Departure**

The case study therefore demonstrates how decisions made in one project area can affect performance in another.
